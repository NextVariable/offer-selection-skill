# Example 07 — Employment vs. PhD

## Setup

- Offers: **School A** — a taught, course-based economics/management master's
  with strong overall brand; **School B** — a research-oriented MRes / MPhil
  (or research-track master's) with a materially stronger research pathway
  (methods training, dissertation, supervision, PhD placement).

Two intents compared.

### Intent A — employment

The employment exit value dominates; School A's brand and recruiting access may
carry more weight.

### Intent B — committed PhD / academic route

Research transformation dominates. The engine asks: "What research candidate
does this offer create, and does it materially improve research capital and
access to the target PhD/academic path?" If School B's research pathway does
that materially, it can outweigh School A's overall rank — because for a
committed PhD intent, *research transformation > overall brand*.

## Expected behavior

The engine first identifies the discipline's training model (PI-driven vs.
programme-driven), then evaluates the route (taught vs. research vs. integrated
PhD) on pathway quality, supervisor/faculty/lab fit, accessible research
opportunity, and historical PhD placement. "Faculty/lab existence does not
guarantee access."

For **uncertain intent**, the engine evaluates academic preparation and the
employment fallback separately — a longer integrated route may be academically
superior but costlier and less flexible.

## What this is NOT

- Not "research always beats employment." The comparison is conditional on the
  user's actual intent.
- Not a U.S.-ecosystem point. A professional, course-only, no-thesis/no-lab/no-RA
  master's receives **no** automatic PhD advantage regardless of country; a
  stronger programme-level research pathway (European research master's, MRes,
  MPhil) dominates it.
- Not a promise of admission probability without defensible data.
