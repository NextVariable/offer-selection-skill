# Example 01 — Zero internships, China-return private sector

## Setup

- Undergraduate: ordinary (双非) institution, business major, middle GPA.
- Offers: **School A** — a recognized name-brand management master's in a
  high-cost global city; **School B** — a same-tier brand in a regional hub
  with China-compatible time zones and accessible internship windows.
- Internships: **zero** relevant experience.
- Goal: return to China for private-sector product roles.
- Budget: both offers fit comfortably.

## Expected behavior

The binding gap is internship + recruiting readiness, not brand. If School B
provides accessible, high-quality internship repair (Shenzhen / mainland
interview access, usable recruiting windows) while School A's timeline makes
recruiting and relevant internship access materially harder, the engine
should lead with School B **for this user**, even though the two brands are
same-tier. It states the mechanism: accessible internship repair outweighs a
limited same-tier brand difference.

The score is **Provisional** because the internship access is an
opportunity-to-verify, not a guaranteed outcome — the engine must not convert
"accessible opportunity" into a confirmed job.

## What this is NOT

- Not "School B is a better school than School A."
- Not a country point (e.g. "HK +N"). The mechanism is *accessible internship
  repair*, which could equally favor a mainland joint-venture or a Singapore
  program in a different pairing.
- The brand gap is called "same-tier" explicitly; the engine does not inflate
  School A's brand to manufacture a close race.

## Reversal

If the student already held several strong relevant internships before
enrolling, the repair value collapses and the outcome shifts toward
equivalence — see Example 02.
