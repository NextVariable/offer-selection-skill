# Local employment and dual track

Country/region feasibility precedes offer comparison because policy, market, language, role structure, and continuity constrain the offer's ceiling. Do not give countries fixed scores.

Evaluate separately:

1. Post-study work access: lawful and usable job-search/work window.
2. Target-role employability: current demand and fit for this user's role, skills, language, and profile.
3. Employer friction: sponsorship, compliance, cost, occupation restrictions, and international hiring willingness.
4. Long-term continuity: viable transition after the first role or post-study period.

Work rights ≠ employability ≠ long-term stay. A generous visa window does not establish a job outcome.

For strong local-stay intent, local employability and employer recognition outrank China-facing rankings. Language may be a risk or gate depending on the role. Research first-job feasibility, current occupation/shortage lists, internship access, and sponsorship transition for the user's expected dates. If every offered path is high-risk or implausible, challenge local stay as the sole criterion, state that conclusion directly, and add a China-return fallback instead of forcing a least-bad country winner.

Treat historical claims about Hong Kong, Japan/Korea, continental Europe, Canada, Ireland, U.S., UK, Australia, and Singapore—including fixed percentages or categorical “only these roles stay”—as priors/search hypotheses only (collected with boundaries in `domain/priors-and-calibration.md`).

For dual track, run complete local and China-return simulations independently. One offer may be locally strong and China-return weak while another is the inverse. Do not blend them unless the user supplies a priority. Preserve fallback and option value. If one offer is easier for the first job while another is stronger for long-term residence, explain both mechanisms and ask which objective controls; do not silently choose for the user.
