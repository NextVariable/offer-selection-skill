## Non-technical

Core: school/credential signal + internship capital + recruiting execution.
Apply the scope and technical-course preference below. A lighter but
well-recognized programme can be rational if it enables recruiting and
internships.

### Major Eligibility Gate scope

For broad private-sector product management, product operations, growth,
marketing, GTM, BD, sales, general operations, commercial, and non-regulated
finance/brokerage roles, the Major Eligibility Gate is **not applicable**. This
file activates the engine's No-major role mode for that scope; no user reminder
is required. Report the gate as "not applicable by the private-sector path
rule" (or "pass by path rule") — a rule consequence, never a claim that
research verified employers accept any major (core: Gates). When the user also
explicitly states that the target roles do not screen by major, treat it as
confirming the operative case constraint.

**Out of scope — the normal Major Eligibility Gate applies** for: technical and
engineering roles; licensed, regulated, clinical, actuarial, quantitative or
otherwise qualification-bound roles; roles covered by a named employer's
published accepted-major directory; and a specific position the user identifies
as carrying a major catalogue. Do not extend this scope to those roles.

Within this scope, do not select, exclude, or penalize offers by degree-title
match, curriculum adjacency, or an assumed industry lock-in. School recognition,
internship capital, recruiting access/readiness, cost and risk remain the main
comparison. A brokerage industry label alone does not activate the SOE path;
use the actual employer/channel to route it.

**Technical-course preference.** When those core factors are materially close,
prefer substantive tech-related subjects (for example, actual programming,
data analysis or technical systems content). This is a positive course
preference, not a major gate, guaranteed hiring advantage, fixed bonus or proof
of product/operations experience. Verify actual content; an AI/Data programme
name alone does not qualify. Other subject areas receive no mismatch penalty.
Actual accessible internships and project outputs may count as role evidence,
but classroom learning must not be relabelled as work experience. Technical
employment itself follows the Technical section below.

**Pre-output check.** Keep every otherwise eligible offer in the comparison
through credential transformation, recruiting access, role evidence, cost/ROI
and risk. Remove any selection or score gap supported only by major-name fit
or assumed vertical lock-in. A technical-course preference must be labelled as
such and cannot override a material difference in the main comparison factors.

Likewise, local post-study work rights are not an automatic advantage on a
clear China-return path. Count them only as option value when the user actually
values and can plausibly exercise a local-stay or dual-track route; otherwise
they are contextual, not a decisive increment for mainland recruiting.

For internship-deficient users, accessible high-quality internship repair can outweigh a limited same-tier brand difference. HK, Singapore, or mainland joint-venture programs may supply local/remote internship access, China-compatible time zones, Shenzhen/mainland interview access, and usable recruiting windows. Verify work rules, schedule, role market, language, and interview requirements. These are opportunities, not guarantees.

For internship-sufficient users, the fourth/fifth ordinary placement has low marginal value. Brand, cost, city, experience, or another gap then matters more. Chinese credential discipline categories usually matter much less than for SOE/public recruiting; verify regulated exceptions.

Assess internship capital by platform, target-role relevance, industry/vertical fit, business line, duration, depth, responsibility, ownership, and measurable output. Three shallow placements may remain a gap; one or two deep relevant placements may suffice.

## Recognition priors (application)

School recognition is target-market-specific and tiered. Conditional country,
school and same-tier examples live only in `domain/priors-and-calibration.md`.
Use core's priors control and the evidence ladder below; do not count the same
recognition signal again under ranking and country.

### Recognition judgment when employer-specific evidence is missing

Credential-recognition value decomposes into two judgments — the market
recognition of the credential in the user's real target market, and the
baseline-dependent marginal upgrade it delivers (core: Gap and
transformation) — never into a fixed point or arithmetic score. Judge
recognition by an evidence ladder, highest first: (1) exact employer-specific
evidence (a named employer's target-school list or explicit ranking
threshold); (2) target-market recognition evidence for the user's real
market; (3) calibrated, market-specific recognition priors
(`domain/priors-and-calibration.md`); (4) generic ranking signal —
supporting evidence only, never mapped to a score.

Absence of an employer-specific school list or ranking threshold does NOT
imply recognition equivalence and must not flatten the pair into "no clear
difference" so that cost alone decides. When exact employer evidence is
unavailable, a market-specific, baseline-dependent credential-recognition
judgment may still establish a directional or material difference using
target-market recognition evidence or calibrated recognition priors. On an
ordinary (双非) undergraduate, a clearly better-recognized master's can be a
material credential upgrade; on a strong undergraduate, the same master's adds
little (core: Gap and transformation). Enter Decision Equivalence (core: Score
and equivalence) only when the available evidence supports the same broad
recognition tier for the user's target market, or when the remaining
difference is unlikely to materially change the user's target-market
competitiveness.

A supported recognition difference prevents manufactured equivalence, but does
not by itself settle the overall recommendation. Stage 5 must still judge
whether its user-specific incremental value justifies the incremental cost.

Recognition differences never override: employer eligibility and Budget hard
gates; a demonstrably repairable binding internship/recruiting gap (accessible
gap repair beats prestige); or programme-level transformation evidence. This
ladder governs private-sector recognition comparisons; SOE/public, PhD/
academic, and local-stay comparisons follow their own owners (core and their
path files) and must not import it. No recognition or ranking arithmetic: no
QS/ranking points or fixed QS30/50/100 thresholds, no US News points, no fixed
school-tier table, no "higher ranking = better recommendation", and no new
scoring weights.

## Technical

Core: school + major credibility + technical training + projects/internships. Raise curriculum depth, prerequisites, lab/project quality, skill formation, discipline strength, and technical outcomes. A rigorous CS/EE/technical program normally beats a vaguely branded technical program that cannot create the required capability. Distinguish a strict eligibility mismatch from a competitiveness weakness.

For U.S. STEM/technical offers, an evidenced ecosystem may support breadth, research-university density, labs/faculty, cross-disciplinary work, technical projects, industry R&D, and internship/research connections — a programme-sensitive prior (see `domain/priors-and-calibration.md`), never universal U.S. superiority. Compare curriculum, rigor, faculty/lab access, project quality, internship accessibility, student outcomes and PhD placement. Local technical employment still requires separate work-access and employability analysis; China return still requires school-specific recognition and credential transformation.

## Network and career support

Network size is not accessible network value. Credit referrals, recruiting access, industry connection, research connection, or target-market opportunity only when the user can realistically convert them. Career-service labels receive no value without demonstrated employer access or outcomes.
